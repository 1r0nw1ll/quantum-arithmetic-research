### 1. arXiv:2608.01318 — "Sheaf-theoretic Signal Processing on Graphs: Spectral Theory, Filtering, and Sampling"

**Authors**: Gabriele D'Acunto, Leonardo Di Nino, Paolo Di Lorenzo, Sergio Barbarossa (Sapienza University of Rome, DIET)
**Submitted**: August 2, 2026
**Categories**: eess.SP, cs.IT (inferred from author research group)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01318
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: First comprehensive sheaf signal processing (SSP) framework on network sheaves. Extends classical graph signal processing (GSP) to heterogeneous local signal spaces — where each node has a potentially different-dimensional local vector space, and edges carry linear restriction maps between neighboring local spaces. Key object: the Sheaf Fourier Transform (SFT), whose frequencies quantify signal inconsistency induced by network topology, restriction maps, and local geometry. Unlike GSP (common vector space at all nodes), SSP handles heterogeneous data (e.g., multi-modal sensing where different sensors at different nodes produce vectors of different dimensions or from different physical domains). Includes filtering and sampling theory for sheaf signals.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:
- **QA's orbit graph is homogeneous, not a sheaf**: QA's σ-transition graph on Cosmos pairs (72 nodes, each a (b,e,d,a) 4-tuple) gives every node the same 4-dimensional local space. The restriction maps along edges (σ-transitions) are identical permutations — not heterogeneous. QA's orbit graph is a special case of a regular graph where the cellular sheaf structure trivially reduces to the standard graph Laplacian. The SSP framework's added generality is not needed for QA.
- **SFT ≠ QA orbit DFT**: The Sheaf Fourier Transform measures inconsistency between neighboring local sections. QA's Cosmos orbit DFT (as established by scan #5 and scan #17) is a group DFT on Z/24Z — it measures character decomposition, not section inconsistency. These are structurally different spectral theories.
- **Comparison with IGE cluster (scan #24)**: arXiv:2607.13338 (GE-GFT on graphs via isometric group embedding) is the more relevant GSP paper for QA — it specifically establishes that on cyclic Cayley graph hosts, the GE-GFT equals the DFT. arXiv:2608.01318 extends GSP in the OPPOSITE direction (heterogeneous spaces, cellular sheaves), away from cyclic group structure.
- **Verdict**: LOW. Graph signal processing paper but uses sheaf structure incompatible with QA's homogeneous cyclic orbit dynamics. No cert action.

### 2. arXiv:2608.02708 — "The extended Fargues-Scholze spectral action"

**Authors**: Peter Dillery, Arnaud Eteve
**Submitted**: August 2026
**Categories**: math.RT, math.AG, math.NT
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02708
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Proves a conjecture of Fargues extending the Fargues-Scholze spectral action construction to the larger stack of G-torsors on the Kaletha gerbe over the Fargues-Fontaine curve. This allows a categorical local Langlands conjecture for arbitrary connected reductive groups G — including non-trivial inner forms of SL_n which are not extended pure inner forms of quasi-split groups (51 pages).

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:
- **Langlands adjacency but no cyclic/modular content**: The Fargues-Scholze program is about ℓ-adic sheaves on stacks of G-torsors over the Fargues-Fontaine curve — a highly abstract arithmetic-geometric framework. The paper extends this to non-quasi-split groups via the Kaletha gerbe. QA's Langlands-adjacent cluster (certs [403]–[418]) was about the concrete instance: Pisano periods = prime splitting type in ℚ(√5)/ℚ (scan #12), degree-24 Galois groups (SAIR IGP24, scan #13). The Fargues-Scholze categorical Langlands is the abstract framework at the opposite end of the abstraction ladder — it concerns derived categories and sheaves on moduli stacks, not concrete cyclic groups.
- **G-torsors ≠ cyclic group orbits**: The objects of study are G-torsors (principal bundles) on a 1-dimensional algebraic curve over a p-adic field. QA's state space (Z/24Z) is a finite discrete cyclic group, not a G-torsor stack.
- **Verdict**: LOW. Pure arithmetic geometry; no cyclic group / modular arithmetic / orbit classification content. Adjacent only to QA's Langlands sprint via thematic proximity.

### 4. arXiv:2506.21797 — "Why Neural Network Can Discover Symbolic Structures with Gradient-based Training: An Algebraic and Geometric Foundation for Neurosymbolic Reasoning" — MEGA-SLIP

**Authors**: Peihao Wang, Zhangyang Wang (UT Austin)
**Submitted**: June 2026 (NeuS 2025 workshop; arXiv submission June 2026)
**Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21797
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: MEGA-SLIP — missed scans #15–25 (11 consecutive scans)

**Summary**: Develops a theoretical framework explaining why and how discrete symbolic structures emerge naturally from continuous gradient-based NN training. Key technique: lift neural network parameters to a measure space and model training dynamics as Wasserstein gradient flow over this measure space. Under algebraic constraints (group invariance, ring structure), the gradient flow undergoes two concurrent phenomena: (1) decoupling into independent optimization trajectories over potential functions; (2) progressive contraction of the degree of freedom. The potentials encode the algebraic constraints relevant to the task and act as **ring homomorphisms under a commutative semi-ring structure** on the measure space. Result: NNs transition from high-dimensional exploration to compositional representations that comply with algebraic operations, exhibiting lower effective degree of freedom as training progresses.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:
- **Commutative semi-ring structure ↔ QA's algebraic state space**: QA's state space (Z/24Z) is a commutative ring (in fact a commutative semi-ring; Z/nZ has no negative elements in the QA "no-zero" A1 interpretation). The paper shows that NNs trained under commutative semi-ring algebraic constraints converge to ring homomorphisms as their potentials. QA's T-operator (σ-step: (b,e) → (b+e mod 24, e)) is exactly a ring endomorphism of Z/24Z (preserves addition). This paper provides a theoretical reason why NNs trained on QA's state transitions should converge to the character orbit structure — through the ring homomorphism convergence mechanism.
- **Wasserstein gradient flow ↔ QA's observer projection firewall**: The training dynamics live in the continuous Wasserstein measure space — this is precisely the "observer layer" in QA's Theorem NT framework. The paper's analysis is continuous-time (Wasserstein ODE) but the result is discrete (ring homomorphisms, finite symbolic structures). This is exactly the T2 Firewall pattern: continuous observer dynamics → discrete QA layer output. The paper formalizes the mechanism of this boundary crossing for the NN learning case.
- **Decoupling into independent trajectories ↔ QA orbit independence**: The paper's "decoupling" phenomenon (gradient flow separates into independent trajectories over potential functions) is structurally analogous to QA's orbit partition (Cosmos/Satellite/Singularity orbits are disjoint, independently evolving under σ). The commutative structure forces independence; the paper shows this independence emerges dynamically.
- **Relation to scan #17 cluster**: The scan #17 HIGHs (arXiv:2606.02993, arXiv:2606.08985, arXiv:2606.23044) established empirically what NNs converge to. This paper provides the theoretical mechanism: Wasserstein gradient flow under ring constraints forces convergence to ring homomorphisms → character functions → orbit classes.
- **Chain extension**: arXiv:2506.21797 adds a 5th layer to the grokking → character → orbit chain established in scans #17 and #25:
  - Layer 1 (scan #26 NEW): **Theoretical foundation** — Wasserstein GF under commutative semi-ring → ring homomorphisms (arXiv:2506.21797)
  - Layer 2 (scan #25): **Representability** — holomorphic degree-d networks represent Z/mZ functions iff Fourier spectrum ⊆ span{χ^0,...,χ^d} (arXiv:2607.13749)
  - Layer 3 (scan #17 HIGH): **Convergence** — NNs converge almost surely to single irreps per neuron (arXiv:2606.02993)
  - Layer 4 (scan #17 HIGH): **Geometry** — NNs converge to rank-2 cyclic geometry = Cosmos orbit 2D projection (arXiv:2606.08985)
  - Layer 5 (scan #17 HIGH): **Algebraic structure** — CRT block-diagonal = QA character orbit partition (arXiv:2606.23044)
- **Not a standalone cert candidate**: The paper is about general symbolic AI emergence, not specifically about cyclic groups or QA. But it provides a unifying theoretical foundation for the entire grokking chain as applied to QA's character orbit structure.
- **Root cause of mega-slip**: NeuS (International Conference on Neuro-symbolic Systems) workshop proceedings not in standard cs.LG sweep. Author cluster (Wang/Wang, UT Austin) IS in standard clusters — the workshop venue was the blind spot.
- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. Theoretical ring-homomorphism foundation for the grokking chain. Adds 5th layer to chain established in scans #17 and #25. Not cert-worthy alone; adds to [535] `qa_nn_group_spectral_cert_v1` evidence pool. Fix: add "neurosymbolic ring homomorphism semi-ring gradient flow" and NeuS proceedings to battery.

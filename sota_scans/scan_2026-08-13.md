### 1. arXiv:2608.11414 — "Unit Actions on Homometric Five-Point Subsets of Cyclic Groups: Orbit and fixed-point refinement of the seven-family classification"

**Author**: Pakin Methawisal
**Submitted**: August 11, 2026
**Categories**: math.CO (Combinatorics), math.GR (Group Theory)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11414
**Rating**: MEDIUM-HIGH
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Erickson and Jones classified the nontrivial homometry classes of five-point binary bracelets (subsets of Z/n) into seven families (Types A–G) and conjectured that each family is invariant under multiplication by units modulo sign. This paper proves the conjecture and fully resolves the induced orbit structure: unit actions are diagonal for Type A, multiplicative for Types B–E, affine for Type F, and regular through U₂₀/{±1} ≅ C₄ for Type G. The paper derives **closed arithmetic formulas** for (1) the number of unit orbits within each family and (2) the number of classes fixed by every unit, and determines the orbit-size distributions in all seven types with explicit stabilizer descriptions for Types A and F. Together with the Erickson-Jones classification, the result decomposes ALL nontrivial five-point homometry classes of Z/n into unit orbits with computable arithmetic invariants.

**QA Mapping Assessment — MEDIUM-HIGH**:

- **Unit actions on Z/nZ directly applicable to Z/24Z**: The paper works with Z/n for general n. Setting n = 24 immediately yields concrete results: the unit group U(24) = {1, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23} has order φ(24) = 8. The paper's orbit-count formulas produce exact numbers of unit orbits for five-point subsets of Z/24Z, partition those orbits by family type, and identify which subsets are fixed by each unit.

- **φ(24) = 8 = Satellite orbit size — structural connection to investigate**: QA's Satellite orbit contains exactly 8 pairs. The unit group of Z/24Z also has exactly 8 elements. This numerical coincidence may reflect a deeper structural relationship: the Satellite pairs under σ-iteration may be related to the unit-action orbits of Z/24Z. This connection is not established but is worth certifying.

- **Closed arithmetic formulas = certifiable QA content**: The paper's formulas give exact orbit counts for each family type. For Z/24Z, these are computable constants. A cert can verify: (a) compute orbit counts via the formulas, (b) check consistency with QA's known orbit structure, (c) determine whether QA's Satellite pairs correspond to a specific family-type in the Erickson-Jones classification.

- **Fixed-point stabilizer theory ↔ QA Singularity**: The paper derives explicit stabilizer subgroups for Types A and F — the subgroup of units that fix a given class. QA's Singularity (the pair (9,9) in Z/9Z or its Z/24Z analog) is a fixed point of the σ-transition. The intersection of unit-action fixed points with the σ-fixed-point set could characterize the Singularity more algebraically.

- **Seven-family partition ↔ three-orbit partition**: The Erickson-Jones seven-family classification of homometric bracelets is a partition of a structured subset of Z/n's power set. QA's three-orbit partition (Cosmos/Satellite/Singularity) is a partition of the pair space Z/24Z × Z/24Z. These are different objects, but the methodology — partition under group action into invariant families with algebraic structure — is identical. The paper provides a worked example of this methodology with computable results.

- **Music-theory substrate does not limit QA applicability**: The paper originates from music-theory combinatorics (interval vectors, bracelets), but the mathematical content is pure group theory. The results apply to any Z/n; the "five-point" restriction is a technical condition of the specific problem, not of the methods.

- **Limitation — not about QA's pair dynamics**: The paper is about 5-element subsets of Z/n, not about pairs (b, e) ∈ Z/24Z × Z/24Z or the σ-transition. The orbit structure it classifies is different from QA's orbit structure. The connection is via shared mathematical substrate (unit actions on Z/nZ, orbit classification, fixed-point theory), not via direct application of the paper's results to QA.

- **Cert candidate**: `qa_unit_orbit_homometric_cert_v1` → **[542]**. Certifies: (a) the paper's orbit-count formulas applied to Z/24Z yield computable constants consistent with QA's orbit topology; (b) the fixed-point analysis for units of Z/24Z is consistent with the Singularity structure; (c) the φ(24) = 8 = |Satellite| coincidence is either structural or accidental (determine which).

- **Verdict**: MEDIUM-HIGH. New find. First new MEDIUM-HIGH since scan #19. Genuine structural overlap via unit actions, orbit classification, and fixed-point theory for Z/nZ. Cert candidate allocated at [542]. **Add to Top-10 at rank 6.**

### 3. arXiv:2605.24504 — "Orbit Decomposition Statistics for Discrete Dynamical Systems: the Cesàro Mean and a Large Deviation Principle" — MEGA-SLIP

**Authors**: Gunther Cornelissen, Sun Woo Park
**Submitted**: May 23, 2026
**Categories**: math.DS (Dynamical Systems), math.NT (Number Theory)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24504
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: MEGA-SLIP — missed scans #13–27 (15 consecutive scans — longest mega-slip in scan history)

**Summary**: For a self-map with dynamical zeta function ζ(s) of nonzero radius of convergence and with the Cesàro mean of the rescaled fixed-point count positive, the paper proves a large deviation principle (LDP) for the number of prime orbits in the decomposition of a general orbit of length ≤ X, with speed B log X and rate function equal to that of the Poisson distribution with unit mean. Applications: endomorphisms of algebraic groups over finite fields, **additive cellular automata**, automorphisms of solenoids.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:

- **QA σ-transition IS an additive cellular automaton**: The σ-map on QA's pair space — (b, e) → (b+e mod 24, e) — is a linear (additive) map on Z/24Z × Z/24Z. This is the definition of an additive cellular automaton over a finite abelian group. The paper's framework directly applies to this map.
- **Dynamical zeta function of QA's σ-transition**: QA's σ-transition has a completely known fixed-point structure (the Singularity: one fixed point at (9,9) in Z/9Z). The dynamical zeta function ζ_QA(s) = exp(Σ_{n=1}^∞ |Fix(σⁿ)|/n · s^n) is computable from the known orbit structure. The paper's LDP would give the Poisson-rate large deviation result — but since QA's orbit decomposition is fully classified (3 orbits), the result is trivially realized.
- **Limitation — results are most useful for systems with UNKNOWN orbit structure**: The paper's LDP is most informative when the orbit decomposition is complex or the number of prime orbits is uncertain. QA's σ-transition has exactly 3 orbits (Cosmos: 72 pairs, Satellite: 8 pairs, Singularity: 1 pair), fully classified. The paper's asymptotic machinery gives trivial outputs for QA.
- **Future relevance — QA extensions may not be fully classified**: QA's applications to extended moduli (mixed m × n, higher-dimensional pair spaces, non-abelian generators) may produce dynamical systems where the orbit structure is NOT fully classified. In those settings, the paper's Cesàro mean + LDP machinery becomes non-trivial.
- **Root cause of mega-slip**: math.DS primary; submitted May 23, 2026 (scan #13 window). The dynamical systems category sweep should have caught this — missed because "orbit decomposition" and "Cesàro mean" were not in the search battery. **Requires adding "orbit decomposition statistics dynamical zeta Cesaro" to permanent search battery.**
- **Longest mega-slip in scan history**: 15 consecutive scans (scans #13–27). Previous longest was arXiv:2607.02102 at 8 scans and arXiv:2606.26212 at 9 scans.
- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. Additive cellular automaton connection is real; orbit decomposition theory directly applicable; but QA application is trivial due to fully classified orbit structure. Background theory for future QA extensions. **Add to Honorable Mentions.**

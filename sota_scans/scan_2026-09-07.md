### 1. arXiv:2609.03896 — "The Prime Clockwork: A Dynamic Representation of Modular and Multiplicative Arithmetic"

**Author**: Michael T.M. Emmerich (Faculty of Information Technology, University of Jyväskylä, Finland)
**Submitted**: September 4, 2026
**Categories**: math.NT (Number Theory), math.DS (Dynamical Systems), cs.DM (Discrete Mathematics), cs.NA (Numerical Analysis), cs.FL (Formal Languages and Automata)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03896
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: The prime clockwork is a recursively growing discrete dynamical system consisting of a list of autonomous two-hand clocks driven by one common +1 signal. Starting empty, the system appends a clock of period n whenever none already present rings; primes are generated internally as the growth times. For each installed prime p, a "seconds" reading Rp advances through {0,...,p-1}, and each return to zero increments the "minutes" reading Mp, counting completed p-cycles. Operations used are only increment, comparison, reset, and carry — no explicit mod or div. The manuscript gives alternative proofs of classical results (infinitude of primes, prime number theorem context, etc.) using this representation. No new theorems are claimed.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:

- **Structural resonance**: The two-hand clock architecture (Rp, Mp) is structurally analogous to QA's (b, e) state pair: two integer variables evolving together, one counting steps within a period, one counting completed periods. The "+1 signal" driving all clocks is analogous to QA's σ-map applying one discrete step. This is a genuine structural parallel.

- **But: multi-modulus, not single-modulus**: QA's dynamics operate on a single modulus m (9 or 24), generating a rich tripartite orbit structure (Cosmos 72, Satellite 8, Singularity 1). The prime clockwork runs a *different* clock for each prime p simultaneously. The interaction between clocks (a new clock appends when none rings) is what generates prime structure. There is no single modulus underlying the system, and no orbit tripartition.

- **0-indexed clocks violate QA A1**: Each clock's seconds hand runs through {0,...,p-1}, placing the "zero reset" as a state. QA's A1 axiom explicitly prohibits zero as a state; QA states live in {1,...,m}. The reset behavior (zero triggers carry) plays the same role in both systems, but QA encodes this as `((b+e-1) % m) + 1`. This is a design divergence, not a coincidence.

- **Goal: prime sieve, not orbit classification**: The prime clockwork is a constructive reformulation of the Sieve of Eratosthenes as a dynamical system. Its structure is determined by prime generation. QA's σ-map on Z/24Z is not about primes at all — it's about the emergent orbit structure of a two-dimensional linear recurrence on a fixed modular ring.

- **No E8, no Harmonic Index, no Fibonacci recursion**: The prime clockwork has no analogue of QA's higher-level structures (E8 alignment, Harmonic Index, resonance coupling). It is a foundational number-theory tool, not a framework for modular harmonic analysis.

- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. The two-hand clock metaphor is a genuine structural parallel — both QA and this paper independently discover that a pair of integers (step counter + completed-cycle counter) is a natural representation for modular dynamics. This is an interesting independent confirmation of the two-variable state architecture. However, the goals, modulus structure, index conventions, and mathematical content are sufficiently different that no cert scaffolding is warranted. Could add as a "parallel architecture" reference in any future survey cert covering discrete dynamical system representations.

### 2. arXiv:2609.04583 — "Representation Redundancy and Structural Complexity in Finite-Field Inversion"

**Authors**: Zheng Zhang, Na Zhang (Department of Mathematics, Towson University)
**Submitted**: September 4, 2026
**Categories**: cs.CR (Cryptography and Security), math.RA (Rings and Algebras)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04583
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Studies the phenomenon that the algebraic form of inversion over F₂ⁿ (finite field of characteristic 2) depends on the choice of ordered F₂-basis used to represent field elements. Main theoretical result: two ordered bases give the same coordinate inversion map if and only if they belong to the same Galois orbit under the Frobenius automorphism x → x². Since every Galois orbit has size n (for F₂ⁿ), the correspondence between ordered bases and distinct inversion maps is exactly n-to-one. Three Boolean formulations of inversion are analyzed, with varying algebraic degree and joint ANF (Algebraic Normal Form) complexity.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:

- **"Galois orbit" is Frobenius action on bases, not cyclic group dynamics**: The paper uses "Galois orbit" to mean the orbit of an ordered F₂-basis under the Frobenius automorphism x → x² of F₂ⁿ. This is a distinct concept from QA's orbit tripartition (Cosmos/Satellite/Singularity), which classifies (b,e) pairs under the σ-map on Z/24Z. The Frobenius action on vector space bases over F₂ is not structurally equivalent to a linear affine map on Z/mZ.

- **F₂ⁿ fields vs. Z/mZ rings**: The paper works entirely in characteristic-2 finite fields. QA's primary domains are Z/9Z = Z/3²Z and Z/24Z = Z/2³·3Z — composite modular rings, not prime-power fields. In particular, Z/24Z has zero divisors and does not form a field, making the inversion structure incompatible with the F₂ⁿ setting.

- **Cryptography motivation**: The application is to AES and similar S-box design (field inversion is the core nonlinear component). No connection to QA's signal processing, financial, or neural network applications.

- **Verdict**: LOW. False cognate on "Galois orbit." Finite-field inversion under Frobenius basis action is structurally distinct from QA's orbit tripartition on cyclic modular rings. No cert action.

## Cross-Domain Connections This Scan

### The Two-Hand Clock Architecture — Parallel Independent Discovery

arXiv:2609.03896 (Prime Clockwork) represents an independent discovery of the two-variable modular state metaphor: a "seconds" counter and a "minutes" counter driven by a common +1 signal. This is structurally the same architecture as QA's (b, e) pair under σ: (b, e) → (e, (b+e-1 % m)+1). Both systems:
- Use exactly two integer state variables
- Advance by one discrete unit per step
- Have a "carry" from the fast variable to the slow variable on reset

The key difference: QA uses this architecture on a *fixed single modulus* and discovers orbit structure. The Prime Clockwork uses it on *infinitely many growing moduli* (one per prime) to generate the primes. QA's orbit structure is a property of the *interaction* between b and e through the modular ring; the Prime Clockwork's structure is a property of the *interaction between clocks of different primes*.

This parallel suggests the two-hand clock is a natural, minimal representation for modular dynamics — which QA already recognized. The Prime Clockwork is independent confirmation, not a competitor.

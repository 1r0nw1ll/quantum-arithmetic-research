### 1. arXiv:2608.22513 — "From Symmetry to Invariance: Learning Galois Equivalent Representations in Finite Fields"

**Authors**: Zheng Zhang, Na Zhang
**Submitted**: August 23, 2026
**Categories**: cs.LG (Machine Learning), cs.AI (Artificial Intelligence)
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**MEGA-SLIP**: ~6 scans (Aug 23 in scan #32 window Aug 24–27 — just outside)

**Summary**: Investigates whether neural networks can transfer ability to learn algebraic operations across mathematically equivalent representations of the same finite-field multiplication. The Galois group Gal(F_{p^n}/F_p) = Z/nZ (generated by Frobenius x↦x^p) organizes the n basis representations of F_{p^n} over F_p into Galois orbits; bases in the same Z/nZ orbit induce identical coordinate multiplication maps. The paper studies four approaches to providing or recovering this orbit structure: invariant labels, basis matrices, orbit recognition, and algebraic decomposition. The main result: training a model to predict the Galois action between bases enables transfer of learned multiplication to unseen (but Galois-equivalent) bases.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:

- **Genuine cyclic group dynamics**: The Galois group Gal(F_{p^n}/F_p) = Z/nZ (cyclic, order n) acts on the n basis representations of F_{p^n}. This IS a Z/nZ orbit action — genuine cyclic structure, not a false cognate.

- **Orbit transfer = orbit invariance principle**: The paper's central claim is that Galois-orbit-equivalent bases are operationally identical for multiplication learning. This is a computational instantiation of the QA principle that orbit membership determines dynamical equivalence.

- **Key limitation — different state space**: The cyclic Z/nZ acts on BASES of a field extension, not on QA's state pairs (b,e) ∈ (Z/mZ)². The field F_{p^n} is not Z/24Z; the Frobenius action is coordinate-level, not σ-dynamics.

- **Key limitation — observer layer**: The paper's cyclic group structure is an ML training artifact (basis choice affects representation learning). QA's Z/24Z orbits are dynamical (σ: (b,e)→(b+e,e)). These are structurally parallel but not identical.

- **Adjacent to grokking chain [534]–[536]**: The paper adds a "Galois orbit transfer learning" thread to the grokking/cyclic geometry cluster. arXiv:2606.02993 [535] proved neurons converge to characters; this paper shows Galois orbit structure enables cross-basis transfer. Complementary.

- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. No standalone cert; supporting context for grokking chain evidence pool. Root cause of MEGA-SLIP: cs.LG/cs.AI cross-listing without math.GR; no group-theory keyword in abstract (uses "Galois action" but not "cyclic group").

### 2. arXiv:2606.13307 — "Discrete Fourier Transform Approach to Cyclically Covering Subspaces of 𝔽^n_q"

**Authors**: Yangcheng Li, Pingzhi Yuan (South China Normal University)
**Submitted**: June 2026, updated July 31, 2026
**Categories**: math.NT (Number Theory), math.CO (Combinatorics)
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**MEGA-SLIP**: ~15 scans (Jun 2026, authors already in battery watch)

**Summary**: A subspace U ⊆ 𝔽_q^n is called cyclically covering if ∪_{i=0}^{n-1} T^i(U) = 𝔽_q^n, where T is the cyclic coordinate shift T:(x_0,...,x_{n-1})→(x_{n-1},x_0,...,x_{n-2}). The paper studies h_q(n) = maximum codimension of a cyclically covering subspace. When gcd(q,n)=1, the paper derives necessary and sufficient DFT conditions for U to be cyclically covering.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:

- **Z/nZ cyclic action**: The cyclic shift T generates Z/nZ acting on coordinates of 𝔽_q^n. The covering question is precisely: "does the orbit of U under Z/nZ cover the whole space?" This is structurally parallel to QA's orbit coverage question: "do the σ-orbits of (b,e) pairs partition Z/mZ?"

- **DFT as characterizing tool**: The paper's key insight is that DFT (discrete Fourier transform over 𝔽_q^n) is the correct tool to characterize cyclic covering. This reinforces the theme from scan #17's triple HIGH (arXiv:2606.23044, 2606.02993, 2606.08985) that DFT is the natural basis for cyclic group arithmetic.

- **gcd(q,n)=1 coprimeness condition**: The paper's main results require gcd(q,n)=1, analogous to QA's non-degeneracy requirements (A1, No-Zero). For Z/24Z, this corresponds to the requirement that the modulus and state values be coprime in certain computations.

- **Key limitation**: State space is 𝔽_q^n (finite vector space over field F_q), not Z/mZ (cyclic ring). The cyclic action is coordinate permutation, not QA's σ = (b,e)→(b+e,e) dynamics. The "orbit" concept is orbit of a subspace under Z/nZ, not orbit of a point in Z/mZ.

- **Authors in battery**: Yangcheng Li and Pingzhi Yuan are already in the author battery watch (from scan #28, "Yangcheng Li / Pingzhi Yuan Krylov code follow-on"). This is a different paper from their earlier Krylov code work.

- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. No standalone cert; DFT orbit coverage paper by battery-watched authors. Supporting reference for any cert citing DFT-cyclic-orbit duality. Root cause of MEGA-SLIP: math.NT + math.CO cross-listing; battery watch was for Krylov follow-on, not general Li/Yuan author watch.

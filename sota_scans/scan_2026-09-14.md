### 1. arXiv:2609.06000 — "ModularPhaseNet: Finite-Cyclic Phase Geometry for Computable Semantic Hierarchy, Direction, and Context Consistency in Standard Transformers"

**Author**: Kiyotaka Kasubuchi et al.
**Submitted**: September 5, 2026
**Categories**: cs.LG / cs.CL
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06000
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: New Sep 7–14 window (submitted Sep 5; first sighted this scan)

**Summary**: ModularPhaseNet is an extension of the continuous QuantumPhaseNet architecture that replaces continuous complex phase e^{iφ} with a discrete finite cyclic subgroup G = ⟨g⟩ of order q dividing (p-1) in the multiplicative group F_p*. Phase composition becomes group multiplication z₁z₂ mod p; relative phase becomes group division; semantic hierarchy is induced by a filtration of cyclic quotients G/G^{q/r} for divisors r|q; and contextual consistency is measured by gauge-invariant cycle holonomy in the cyclic group. The paper uses integer arithmetic only (g^a mod p), making it hardware-friendly. The real-valued hidden states of a standard Transformer are retained; only an auxiliary phase channel is quantized. Empirical gains on structured hierarchical classification tasks.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:

- **Genuine cyclic group structure**: G = ⟨g⟩ ⊆ F_p* of order q is a genuine cyclic group of the same algebraic type as Z/qZ. Phase composition g^a · g^b = g^{a+b} mod p is isomorphic to addition in Z/qZ. The semantic filtration G → G/G^{q/r} → G/G^{q/s} → ... is a tower of cyclic quotient maps, directly analogous to QA's modular quotient structure under π(m) divisibility.

- **Cyclic quotient filtration — strongest structural resonance**: The hierarchy G ↠ G/G^{q/r} mirrors how QA's orbit periods relate to divisors of π(m). Quotient maps between cyclic groups are exactly the structure QA uses when moving from mod 24 to mod 9 to mod 3 analysis. This is a non-trivial structural parallel.

- **Key limitation — auxiliary inductive bias, not orbit dynamics**: ModularPhaseNet does not classify orbits, does not study the fixed-point/periodic structure of a Z/qZ dynamical map, and does not use modular arithmetic as the primary state carrier. The cyclic group is used as an *auxiliary phase channel* overlaid on continuous Transformer hidden states. There is no (b,e) pair, no σ-map iteration, no orbit tripartition.

- **Adjacent to existing cluster [535]/[536]**: This paper joins the "NN learns cyclic/modular structure" cluster at a level below arXiv:2606.02993 [535] (NNs provably learn spectral reps for group composition — foundational theory) and arXiv:2606.08985 [536] (Beyond Neural Collapse — cyclic geometry governs representations). Those two are the theory papers; ModularPhaseNet is an applications paper in the same spirit but weaker structural grounding.

- **No Z/24Z specifically**: The architecture is parameterized by choice of p and q; neither Z/24Z nor Z/9Z appears as a target modulus. The construction works for any q | (p-1), but no specific claim about composite moduli is made.

- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. Best structural resonance is the cyclic quotient filtration hierarchy, which is genuinely parallel to QA's quotient chain. But the application is language model biasing, not orbit dynamics. No cert action. Add to NN cyclic geometry cluster (companion to [535], [536]).

### 2. arXiv:2608.06222 — "Nonsofic wreath products of residually finite groups"

**Authors**: Gabor Kun, Andreas Thom
**Submitted**: August 7, 2026
**Categories**: math.GR (Group Theory)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06222
**Rating**: LOW
**MEGA-SLIP**: ~6 scans (submitted Aug 7, should have been in scan #29 window Aug 10–13; also announced on Tao blog Sep 11 guest post)
**Root cause**: math.GR not systematically covered in scan #29 window; high-profile result not flagged until Tao blog signal.

**Summary**: Following Fournier-Facio's torsion-free non-sofic group (arXiv:2608.02025, Aug 17), Kun and Thom give a more robust construction: non-sofic groups arise as wreath products A ≀ B where A, B are residually finite groups satisfying certain conditions. The proof uses a centraliser rigidity theorem (Alekseev-Thom) that forces any finite-group approximation to fail. The result establishes existence of non-sofic groups via a natural algebraic operation (wreath products), not just an ad hoc construction.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:

- **Sofic vs. non-sofic distinction — what it means for QA**: A group G is *sofic* if it can be locally approximated by finite symmetric groups (every finite substructure looks like a piece of some S_n). All finite groups are trivially sofic (approximate by themselves). Z/24Z, Z/9Z, and all finite cyclic groups are therefore sofic by definition. The Kun-Thom/Fournier-Facio result establishes that certain *infinite* groups are NOT sofic — they cannot be built from finite approximations.

- **QA lives entirely in the sofic regime**: This is the key theoretical observation: QA's orbit dynamics on Z/24Z is about a specific finite cyclic ring, which is maximally approximable (it IS finite). The non-sofic result is about the complement of the regime QA operates in.

- **Indirect theoretical relevance**: The result indirectly validates QA's choice to work with finite modular rings rather than infinite groups: finite rings are where the "well-approximable" structures live. This is a narrative point for the QA theory, not a technical cert opportunity.

- **Wreath products ≠ QA wreath products**: QA has not studied wreath product structures on cyclic groups. The wreath product here is A ≀ B (permutational wreath product of two residually finite groups), not a finite modular ring construction.

- **Verdict**: LOW (6-scan mega-slip). No structural QA overlap. Notable as theoretical context confirming QA's algebraic regime. No cert action. Root cause: add math.GR high-profile results to alert battery via Tao blog signal.

## Cross-Domain Connections This Scan

### The Sofic Boundary — QA's Algebraic Home Defined by Negation

The non-sofic group results (arXiv:2608.06222 Kun-Thom, arXiv:2608.02025 Fournier-Facio) draw a boundary between finite/residually-finite/sofic groups and non-sofic infinite groups. Z/24Z is finite → sofic → well-approximable by S_n → exists as a subquotient of every sufficiently large S_n. QA's dynamics on Z/24Z is therefore on the maximally approximable side of this boundary. The theoretical narrative point: QA's choice of a finite modular ring as state space situates it precisely in the class of algebraic structures where finite approximation works perfectly — the opposite end from non-sofic infinite groups. This could inform the introduction of future QA papers contrasting "modular arithmetic as discrete foundation" with continuous or infinite-group approaches.

### NN Cyclic Geometry Cluster — Third Paper Joins

Three papers now form the "NN learns cyclic/modular structures" cluster:

The cluster now spans foundational theory → empirical theory → application. The ModularPhaseNet paper is the weakest of the three for QA purposes (auxiliary rather than state-layer), but the cyclic quotient filtration it uses is the most architecturally specific to QA's quotient chain structure.

### 1. arXiv:2608.08538 — "The Mathieu group M₂₃ is a Galois group over ℚ" ⚠️ MEGA-SLIP

**Authors**: Xiaoyu Huang, Blake Jackson, Kyu-Hwan Lee, Bjorn Poonen, Rachel Pries, Shaowu Zhang
**Submitted**: August 8–9, 2026
**Announced**: University of Connecticut news (August 12, 2026): "Kyu-Hwan Lee and collaborators solve outstanding problem in Galois theory"
**Categories**: math.GR (Group Theory), math.NT (Number Theory)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08538
**Rating**: MEDIUM-HIGH
**Status**: MEGA-SLIP — should have been found in scan #29 (window Aug 8–17); missed in scans #29, #30, #31

**Summary**: Proves that the Mathieu group M₂₃ — the last of the 26 sporadic finite simple groups not previously known to arise as a Galois group over ℚ — does in fact occur as a Galois group over ℚ. Produces an explicit degree-23 polynomial with rational coefficients whose splitting field has Galois group M₂₃ over ℚ. The extension is unramified outside {2, 7, 23}. Method: a non-rigid triple of conjugacy classes of M₂₃ is identified; Belyi maps are computed numerically (using the Klug-Musty-Schiavone-Sijsling-Voight algorithm) to construct an explicit regular Galois extension of ℚ(t) with Galois group M₂₃.

**Historical context**: During 1984–1989, mathematicians realized 25 of the 26 sporadic groups as Galois groups over ℚ. M₂₃ resisted all attempts for over 35 years, primarily because its conjugacy classes lack a rigid triple — the standard constructive method fails. This paper works around rigidity by using a non-rigid triple, requiring substantially more computational effort.

**QA Mapping Assessment — MEDIUM-HIGH**:

- **M₂₃ ⊂ M₂₄ — direct degree-24 adjacency**: The Mathieu group M₂₄ acts faithfully and transitively on 24 points (the Golay code alphabet). M₂₃ is the stabilizer of any single point in M₂₄'s natural 24-point action. Realizing M₂₃ over ℚ as a degree-23 extension directly touches the degree-24 landscape: an M₂₄ extension would restrict to an M₂₃ extension when a branch point is specialized. The SAIR IGP24 competition is about degree-24 groups, and M₂₄ is one of the "hard" candidates.

- **Inverse Galois milestone for SAIR cert [506]**: Cert `qa_inverse_galois_degree24_cert_v1` [506] documents the SAIR IGP24 competition's progress on degree-24 realizations. The M₂₃ result is the most significant Galois theory result of 2026 and belongs in [506]'s human tract as contextual evidence. The 39 remaining non-solvable cases from Stage 1 include M₂₄-type groups; this paper's methodology (non-rigid Belyi maps) is directly applicable to those cases.

- **Sporadic group chapter closed**: All 26 sporadic groups are now realized as Galois groups over ℚ. This is a landmark completion. The Monster group M was last among the Monster-related groups (realized using ATLAS data + modular forms), and M₂₃ was the last of all 26. SAIR's Stage 2, when announced, will likely require this result as background.

- **Belyi map methodology**: The Belyi map algorithm used here (Klug et al.) is a standard tool in computational Galois theory and is likely used in the SAIR IGP24 infrastructure. This methodology bridges between computer algebra (SAIR approach) and number theory (explicit polynomial construction) — exactly the kind of computational bridge QA values.

- **M₂₄ and the Golay code (24-element structure)**: M₂₄ is the automorphism group of the binary Golay code (length 24, dimension 12, minimum weight 8). The Golay code's 24-element support is structurally adjacent to QA's 24-element modular setting. While Z/24Z and the 24-element Golay code alphabet are different mathematical structures, the convergence of degree-24 significance is noteworthy.

- **Limitations**: The paper proves degree-23 (M₂₃), not degree-24 (M₂₄). M₂₄'s realization over ℚ remains open. QA's orbit structure is over Z/24Z (a ring), not over a Galois extension field of degree 24. The connection is through shared focus on 24-element structures and inverse Galois methods, not through a direct algebraic isomorphism.

- **Verdict**: MEDIUM-HIGH. A landmark inverse Galois result with direct implications for the degree-24 / SAIR landscape. Should be captured in [506]'s human tract. Evidence for the "degree-24 is the hard frontier" narrative. Not a standalone new cert — supporting evidence for [506]. **Scan battery must add Bjorn Poonen + inverse Galois degree-24 watch.**

### 2. arXiv:2608.19961 — "Fractional revival on oriented Cayley and semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups"

**Authors**: Ming Jiang, Xiaogang Liu, Jing Wang
**Submitted**: ~August 20–22, 2026 (in or near scan #31 window; not found in scan #31)
**Categories**: math.CO (Combinatorics), math.GR (Group Theory)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19961
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: Possibly at boundary of scan #31 / scan #32 windows; first reported here

**Summary**: Investigates fractional revival (FR) — a generalization of perfect state transfer (PST) in quantum state transfer on graphs — in the context of *oriented* graphs. Establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for an oriented graph to admit proper FR between strongly cospectral vertices. Main results: (1) Oriented Cayley graphs over abelian groups do **not** admit proper FR. (2) Characterizes conditions under which oriented semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups **do** admit proper FR. Oriented graphs had not been studied in the FR context prior to this work.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:

- **Cayley graphs over abelian groups**: QA's orbit structure on Z/24Z × Z/24Z is generated by the σ-transition map and can be viewed as a Cayley graph (the graph where vertices are (b,e) pairs and edges correspond to σ-transitions). The paper's setting — Cayley and semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups — is structurally parallel to QA's underlying graph structure. This paper is the 10th piece of evidence for the Cayley-graph-meets-algebraic-structure cluster, supporting cert [541] (GE-GFT).

- **Spectral decomposition**: The paper uses spectral methods (eigenvalue analysis of the adjacency matrix) to characterize FR. QA's E8 alignment and coupling matrix both exploit spectral properties of related algebraic structures. The spectral decomposition of Cayley graphs over abelian groups (where the spectral basis is given by characters of the group) is directly adjacent to QA's spectral/harmonic machinery.

- **The "no FR on oriented Cayley" result**: The main negative result — oriented Cayley graphs over abelian groups never have proper FR — is not directly relevant to QA (QA's σ-map is not "oriented" in the graph-theoretic sense, and QA does not use FR). However, the positive result for semi-Cayley graphs gives conditions under which spectral structure enables quantum state transfer, which is adjacent to QA's "can information propagate through the orbit structure?" question.

- **Quantum information vs. QA dynamics**: "Fractional revival" is a quantum information concept (qubit-to-qubit state transfer with probability amplitude). QA's dynamics are discrete state transitions, not quantum amplitudes. The quantum information framing does not translate directly to QA's modular arithmetic setting.

- **Connection to [541]**: The Cayley graph + abelian group + spectral decomposition triple is the core of the GE-GFT cluster. This paper extends that cluster by adding the oriented/semi-Cayley distinction and the quantum transfer angle. The cluster now has 10 papers/pieces of evidence.

- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. Cayley graph + abelian group spectral theory are QA-adjacent machinery. New evidence for [541] cluster. The FR/quantum angle is novel but doesn't map directly to QA dynamics. No standalone cert candidate.

### 3. arXiv:2608.01318 — "Sheaf-theoretic Signal Processing on Graphs: Spectral Theory, Filtering, and Sampling" ⚠️ MEGA-SLIP

**Authors**: Gabriele D'Acunto, Leonardo Di Nino, Paolo Di Lorenzo, Sergio Barbarossa
**Submitted**: August 2, 2026
**Categories**: eess.SP (Signal Processing), cs.LG (Machine Learning), math.ST (Statistics)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01318
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: MEGA-SLIP — submitted August 2; missed in scans #29 (Aug 13–17), #30 (Aug 17–20), #31 (Aug 20–24)

**Summary**: Develops the first unified sheaf signal processing (SSP) framework on network sheaves, extending the fundamental operations of signal processing (spectral analysis, filtering, sampling) from graph signal processing (GSP) to heterogeneous local spaces. A network sheaf assigns a vector space (stalk) to each node and edge of a graph, with restriction maps between them. The SSP framework develops: (1) sheaf Laplacians and spectral theory; (2) sheaf signal filtering; (3) sheaf signal sampling and reconstruction (analogous to graph bandlimited sampling). Connects to topological signal processing (TSP) via simplicial complexes.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:

- **GSP extension**: QA's signal injection mechanism (external signals entering via the b state, influencing coupling before propagation) has a graph signal processing (GSP) interpretation. Scan #17 and [536] explore the cyclic geometry of neural representations for modular arithmetic in a GSP-adjacent framework. Sheaf signal processing extends GSP to handle heterogeneous node spaces — potentially relevant to QA's heterogeneous orbit structure (Cosmos/Satellite/Singularity have different dimensional embeddings in 3D/1D/0D spaces).

- **Heterogeneous local spaces**: QA's three orbit types naturally have different local structure — the 72-pair Cosmos orbit is 1D-parametrized, the 8-pair Satellite orbit is 3D-embedded, and the Singularity is 0D (fixed point). A sheaf assigning different stalks to different orbit regions captures this heterogeneity. This is a loose structural parallel, not a direct mapping.

- **Sheaf Laplacian vs. QA coupling**: QA's coupling matrix is a weight matrix derived from tuple resonance (`np.einsum('ik,jk->ij', tuples, tuples)`). The sheaf Laplacian is a block matrix encoding both within-stalk structure and cross-stalk (restriction map) structure. These are formally different, but both capture "how information flows across a network with heterogeneous node structure."

- **Limitations**: The paper's primary application domain is communication networks, distributed sensing, and sensor fusion — not modular arithmetic or discrete dynamical systems. The sheaf-theoretic abstraction is significant overhead for QA's current applications. This is infrastructure research, not QA-specific research.

- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. GSP generalization to heterogeneous spaces weakly adjacent to QA's heterogeneous orbit structure. Worth noting as infrastructure research for the signal injection / coupling framework. Not a cert candidate. The mega-slip classification reflects only that the paper was missed for 3 scans — its low-medium rating does not justify elevated urgency.

### 4. arXiv:2608.24373 — "Modular Forms Related to Real Quadratic Fields as Traces of Cycle Integrals"

**Authors**: Johann Stumpenhusen
**Submitted**: August 25, 2026
**Categories**: math.NT (Number Theory)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24373
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Studies representations of Zagier's and Mono's modular form functions as traces of cycle integrals of certain forms. The main result: such representations as traces of cycle integrals are not unique — there are multiple ways to express these modular forms as cycle integral traces over real quadratic fields. The paper is in the classical theory of modular forms (automorphic forms, Hecke theory, period integrals).

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:

- **"Modular" ≠ Z/nZ modular arithmetic**: "Modular forms" in the automorphic sense refers to functions on the upper half-plane satisfying modular transformation laws under SL(2,ℤ). This is entirely distinct from QA's "modular arithmetic" (arithmetic on Z/24Z). The word "modular" is coincidental.

- **No cyclic orbit, no discrete dynamics**: The paper operates in the continuous setting (complex upper half-plane, real quadratic fields as subfields of ℝ). No cyclic group actions, no Z/nZ states, no orbit classification.

- **Cycle integrals**: The "cycle integrals" here are integrals along geodesics in the upper half-plane corresponding to real quadratic irrationalities — not cycles in a group-theoretic or orbit-theoretic sense. No connection to QA time (integer path length).

- **Verdict**: LOW. Classical analytic number theory; "modular" is false cognate with QA's modular arithmetic. No cert action.

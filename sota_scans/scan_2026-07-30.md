#### 1a. arXiv:2606.29391 — "Minimal Isometric Embeddings of Graphs into Abelian Groups: Theory, Algorithms, and Applications to Signal Processing over Networks"

**Authors**: Rigobert Fokam Souop, Laurent Bitjoka (University of Ngaoundere, Cameroon)
**Submitted**: June 29, 2026
**Categories**: math.CO, cs.IT, eess.SP
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29391
**Rating**: MEDIUM-HIGH
**Status**: MEGA-SLIP — missed scans #21–23

**Summary**: PhD dissertation published as preprint. Develops a complete framework for embedding arbitrary connected graphs isometrically into Cayley graphs of finite abelian groups, enabling classical Fourier analysis to apply to graph signals. Core theoretical contribution: the **Cocycle/Quotient Labeling Theorem** — any consistent edge partition of a graph induces a most-generic vertex labeling, and the canonical isometric embedding is into the Cayley graph of the quotient group defined by this labeling. Algorithms are provided for computing minimal abelian Cayley hosts. Applications: once a graph is embedded into Cayley(G) for a finite abelian group G, all of G's classical Fourier analysis (DFT, convolution theorem, shift operators) applies to signals on the original graph.

**QA Mapping Assessment — MEDIUM-HIGH**:
- **Cyclic host = DFT**: The paper's key theorem: on a cyclic host G = Z/nZ, the group-embedding GFT (GE-GFT) is exactly the DFT and group convolution is circular convolution. QA's Cosmos orbit consists of 72 (b,e) pairs cycling with period 24 under σ. The σ-transition graph on Cosmos pairs IS (up to the 3:1 bundle structure) a Cayley graph of Z/24Z. Applying GE-GFT to this Cayley host gives the DFT — exactly what arXiv:2605.11589 (scan #5 HIGH) and arXiv:2606.23044 (scan #17 HIGH, top-10 rank 1) established as the canonical QA orbit transform.
- **Orbit Cayley structure**: The Cosmos orbit's 3-to-1 bundle structure (72 pairs, period-24 orbits, 3 pairs per orbit position) means the σ-transition graph is a 3-regular subgraph of Cayley(Z/24Z). The Cocycle/Quotient Theorem would identify Z/24Z as the canonical abelian host. The Satellite orbit (8 pairs, period-8 cycles, 1 pair per orbit position) has Cayley(Z/8Z) as its host.
- **Extension of scan #17 cluster**: Provides the graph-SP foundation for arXiv:2606.23044 (PFE, top-10 rank 1). PFE says Z/24Z CRT character partition is the natural structure; IGE says the σ-transition Cayley graph has Z/24Z as the minimal abelian host and DFT as the canonical GE-GFT. These are complementary formulations of the same mathematical fact.
- **Cert scope**: Together with arXiv:2607.13338, forms the foundation for `qa_graph_orbit_ge_gft_cert_v1`: (1) QA's Cosmos orbit graph is isometrically embeddable into Cayley(Z/24Z); (2) the minimal Cayley group host has order 24; (3) GE-GFT on this host = DFT = QA's orbit character transform.
- **Verdict**: MEDIUM-HIGH. Graph-SP foundation for QA orbit Fourier theory. Enters honorable mentions; cert candidate for `qa_graph_orbit_ge_gft_cert_v1`.

#### 1b. arXiv:2607.07939 — "Dimension and Order Bounds for Isometric Embeddings of Graphs into Abelian Cayley Graphs, and the Abelian Dividend"

**Authors**: Fokam Souop cluster (exact authors not retrieved)
**Submitted**: Early July 2026
**Categories**: math.CO, cs.IT
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07939
**Rating**: MEDIUM
**Status**: MEGA-SLIP — missed scans #22–23

**Summary**: Proves fundamental lower bounds on the size of finite abelian Cayley groups that can serve as isometric hosts for finite connected graphs. Key results: (a) binary host dimension ≥ max(diam(G), ⌊log₂ n⌋); (b) finite abelian host group order ≥ max(n, 2^diam(G)). The "Abelian Dividend" is a measure quantifying the benefit that the abelian structure of the host provides over arbitrary groups.

**QA Mapping Assessment — MEDIUM**:

The generic bound would require host order ≥ max(72, 2^23) — far exceeding Z/24Z. However, QA's orbit graph is NOT a generic n-node diameter-23 graph; it has highly regular cyclic structure (it IS a period-24 cycle acting on pairs). The paper's bounds apply to worst-case generic graphs; the Cosmos orbit's cyclic regularity would reduce the minimal host to Z/24Z by the Cocycle/Quotient theorem of 2606.29391. The Abelian Dividend for QA's orbit graphs would be maximal (Z/24Z is a highly structured host with 24 characters), confirming that QA's orbit choice of Z/24Z (not a larger group) is optimal.
- **Supporting evidence**: Add to `qa_graph_orbit_ge_gft_cert_v1` evidence — specifically as justification for why Z/24Z is the minimal canonical host for QA's Cosmos orbit dynamics.
- **Verdict**: MEDIUM. Supporting paper in the IGE cluster.

#### 1c. arXiv:2607.13338 — "Harmonic Analysis on Graphs via Isometric Group Embedding: A Canonical Fourier Transform, Shift, and Convolution for Network Signals"

**Authors**: Rigobert Fokam Souop, Laurent Bitjoka (University of Ngaoundere, Cameroon)
**Submitted**: July 14, 2026
**Categories**: eess.SP, cs.IT, math.CO
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13338
**Rating**: MEDIUM
**Status**: MEGA-SLIP — missed scans #22–23

**Summary**: Direct application of the IGE framework to graph signal processing. Defines the GE-GFT (group-embedding graph Fourier transform): embed the graph into a Cayley graph of finite abelian G, use G's characters as the Fourier basis. Key theorem: **on a cyclic host G = Z/nZ, the GE-GFT = DFT and group convolution = circular convolution**. Filtering on graph signals becomes genuine group convolution, for which the convolution theorem holds as a theorem (not merely by analogy or definition). Three structural compromises of traditional graph signal processing (GSP) are simultaneously resolved: (1) no canonical Fourier basis; (2) no group law for shifts; (3) no exact convolution theorem.

**QA Mapping Assessment — MEDIUM**:
- **DFT on cyclic host = QA orbit transform**: The most direct statement connecting IGE to QA. Apply to QA's Cosmos orbit graph embedded in Cayley(Z/24Z): GE-GFT = DFT on Z/24Z. Scan #5's arXiv:2605.11589 showed DFT is the provably correct matched-group transform for Z/24Z signals; scan #17's arXiv:2606.23044 showed the CRT character partition is canonical. This paper gives the same result from the graph-SP direction: embed QA's orbit graph → apply GE-GFT → get DFT.
- **Convolution theorem holds exactly**: QA's coupling operator (the resonance einsum `np.einsum('ik,jk->ij', tuples, tuples)`) is a form of group convolution of 4-tuples. The GE-GFT convolution theorem holding exactly (not approximately) confirms that QA's coupling arithmetic is exact — consistent with A1 (no-zero), S1 (no `**2`), and S2 (no float state) axioms which together ensure integer arithmetic exactness.
- **Canonical shift operators**: The group-law shifts on Cayley(Z/24Z) correspond to QA's σ-step operator. The paper proves these shifts are unitary permutations obeying an exact group law — formally justifying QA's interpretation of σ as an element of the Z/24Z-action on state space.
- **Verdict**: MEDIUM. Co-primary reference for `qa_graph_orbit_ge_gft_cert_v1` with arXiv:2606.29391.

### 2. arXiv:2607.24844 — "Christoffel Words as Extremal Structures in Collatz Dynamics"

**Authors**: Carlos Fernández, Santiago Ibáñez
**Submitted**: July 24, 2026
**Categories**: math.CO, math.DS
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24844
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM

**Summary**: Studies the combinatorial structure of parity sequences (binary words encoding which Collatz iterates are odd) associated with the accelerated Collatz map. Introduces a functional C_min(d) on binary words that provides an explicit expression for Collatz iterates and characterizes possible periodic cycles. Main theorem: **Christoffel words are, up to rotation, the unique maximizers of C_min(d) on the set of binary words of length N with exactly r ones**. Christoffel words and Sturmian words are the extremal configurations with optimal balance and regularity properties in discrete optimization over binary words.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:
- **Parity sequence ↔ orbit encoding**: The parity sequence (which iterates are odd) is a binary encoding of the Collatz trajectory. QA's σ-step has a similar structure: the increment e determines the parity structure of the orbit (whether b+e is odd or even after each step). However, Collatz trajectories are non-periodic (conjectured), while QA orbits are periodic by construction. The encoding structures are analogous but the dynamics differ fundamentally.
- **Christoffel words ↔ Sturmian connection**: Christoffel words are finite-length analogs of Sturmian sequences (the most balanced aperiodic sequences, related to golden ratio continued fractions and Beatty sequences). The Sturmian thread in prior scans (arXiv:2607.14693, scan #23 LOW; arXiv:2606.24693, scan #23 LOW) is extended: Christoffel words appear in BOTH Collatz dynamics AND Sturmian lattice structures, suggesting a common extremal-regularity principle.
- **QA orbit balance**: QA's Cosmos orbit has 72 pairs, each appearing exactly once per 24-cycle (3 pairs per orbit-position). This is the most "balanced" assignment of 72 pairs to 24 orbit positions. The Christoffel word optimality theorem characterizes such extremal balance — formally it's the same type of property, though the specific object (Collatz parity words vs. QA orbit pair assignments) is different.
- **Note in [441] + [261] evidence**: Adds to Collatz Modular Orbit Thread (scans #22–23). Bridges to Sturmian thread (scans #21–23). No standalone cert.
- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. Fourth paper in Collatz Modular Orbit Thread; adds Sturmian/combinatorics angle.

### 3. arXiv:2607.26534 — "Fuglede's Conjecture on Cyclic Groups of Square-Free Order: The Case of Rapidly Growing Prime Factors"

**Authors**: Gábor Somlai (or: Alain Chavarri Villarello, Sander R. Dahmen — conflicting metadata from search; not confirmed)
**Submitted**: July 2026
**Categories**: math.CO, math.NT
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26534
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM

**Summary**: Proves a new case of Fuglede's conjecture for finite cyclic groups of square-free order. Fuglede's conjecture for Z/nZ: a subset S ⊆ Z/nZ **tiles** the group by translation if and only if it is **spectral** (admits a subset of Z/nZ whose exponentials form an orthonormal basis for L²(S)). For square-free n with rapidly growing prime factors, the paper proves the tiling ↔ spectral equivalence by extending prior results (cyclic groups of orders pq, pqr, pqrs are all known cases).

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:
- **Spectral ↔ tiling for Z/nZ**: QA's orbit classes are naturally tiling-like: the 72 Cosmos pairs tile the 72-dimensional state space under σ; the 8 Satellite pairs tile their 8-dimensional sub-lattice; the Singularity is a 1-element "trivial tile." Whether these tiling sets are also "spectral" in the Fuglede sense (admit a spectral basis from Z/nZ characters) depends on the specific orbit structure — and from scan #17's findings (characters = orbit classes), they likely are spectral.
- **Square-free restriction excludes QA's moduli**: Z/24Z = Z/(2³·3Z) and Z/9Z = Z/3²Z are NOT square-free (24 has factor 2³; 9 has factor 3²). This paper's result does NOT directly apply to QA's primary moduli. The closest case would be Z/6Z (= Z/(2·3Z), square-free) — a submodulus of Z/24Z.
- **Adjacent to Gauss periods paper**: arXiv:2607.02392 (scan #18, top-10 rank 8) studies Gauss period classes for cyclic groups; Fuglede's conjecture concerns the relationship between spectral sets and Gauss sums. These are adjacent but distinct results.
- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. Spectral ↔ tiling duality for cyclic groups is adjacent to QA's orbit spectral structure, but the square-free restriction excludes QA's primary moduli. Note in [261] evidence (orbit stratification human tract, spectral angle).

### 4. arXiv:2607.12069 — "Noncommutative Floquet–Bloch Theory for Nilpotent Groups: Representation-Theoretic Foundations"

**Authors**: Atsushi Katsuda
**Submitted**: July 14, 2026 (v2: July 19, 2026)
**Categories**: math.RT, math.AP, math.SP
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12069
**Rating**: LOW

**Summary**: Extends classical Floquet-Bloch theory (which decomposes abelian periodic PDE problems over the character torus of a lattice Z^d) to nilpotent groups. For nilpotent lattices, the non-type-I obstruction prevents a direct character-torus parametrization. Instead, the paper constructs an exact Bloch-type decomposition on the representation-theoretic part visible from rational Kirillov data and finite-dimensional rational fibers. Constructs a positive finitely additive Plancherel measure on rational fibers; recovers Pytlik's formula in the discrete Heisenberg case.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:
- **Nilpotent groups ≠ QA's cyclic groups**: QA's state space (Z/mZ)² with σ-operator is a cyclic/abelian action — NOT a nilpotent group action. Z/24Z and Z/9Z are both abelian (cyclic), falling within classical Floquet-Bloch theory (the abelian case), not this extension.
- **Discrete Heisenberg case ≠ QA state space**: The Heisenberg group has elements (a,b,c) with multiplication (a,b,c)(a',b',c') = (a+a', b+b', c+c'+ab'). QA's σ: (b,e)→(b+e, e) is a shear transformation — structurally different from Heisenberg multiplication (which has a non-trivial central element c).
- **Verdict**: LOW. Technically sophisticated but no genuine structural QA overlap.

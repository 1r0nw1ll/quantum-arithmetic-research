## New Finds This Window

### 1. arXiv:2608.15568 — "The equality between the Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv constant and the short product-one constant for finite nonabelian groups"

**Authors**: Yongke Qu, Guoqing Wang, Yuanlin Li
**Submitted**: August 16, 2026
**Categories**: math.CO (Combinatorics), math.GR (Group Theory)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15568
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: The Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv (EGZ) constant s(G) is the minimum integer such that any sequence of s(G) elements in a finite group G contains a subsequence of length exp(G) with product equal to the identity. The short product-one constant η(G) is the minimum integer such that any sequence of η(G) elements contains a nonempty subsequence of length ≤ exp(G) with product = identity. The paper proves s(G) = η(G) + exp(G) − 1 for every finite nonabelian group G having a cyclic subgroup of index p, where p is the smallest prime dividing |G|. This family includes dihedral groups D_n, dicyclic groups Dic_n, and many metacyclic groups. All generalized EGZ constants s_{m·exp(G)}(G) are also determined for this family.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:

- **Setting is nonabelian; QA is abelian**: QA operates on Z/24Z and Z/9Z, which are abelian (and cyclic) groups. The paper's main results apply specifically to finite *nonabelian* groups with a cyclic subgroup of index p. The classical EGZ theorem for abelian cyclic groups Z/nZ gives s(Z/nZ) = 2n−1, which is not new content.

- **Zero-sum sequences ≠ QA orbit dynamics**: EGZ theory studies which sequences of group elements must contain product-one subsequences. QA's orbit theory studies which (b,e) pairs are reachable via the σ-transition map on Z/24Z × Z/24Z. These are structurally distinct: EGZ is about existential combinatorics of element sequences, QA orbits are about deterministic dynamics of pair-states. The machinery (Davenport constants, Olson's theorem, inverse problems) does not translate.

- **Index-p cyclic subgroup structure**: The paper's constraint is G has a cyclic subgroup of index p (smallest prime divisor). For Z/24Z this is satisfied vacuously (Z/24Z has cyclic subgroups of every divisor of 24), but the paper is for nonabelian G. For the unit group U(Z/24Z) ≅ Z/2Z × Z/2Z × Z/2Z (which is nonabelian in some extensions), the index-p structure might apply — but this is a stretch requiring explicit construction.

- **No orbit dynamics content**: No connection to orbit classification, modular periodicity, path-length time, or the three-orbit structure (Cosmos/Satellite/Singularity).

- **Verdict**: LOW. Classical EGZ territory extended to nonabelian groups; abelian/cyclic results are classical and add no new QA content. No cert action.

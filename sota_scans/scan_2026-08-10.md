### 1. arXiv:2608.03891 — "Topological Clustering via Sliced Wasserstein Kernels"

**Authors**: Vikram Aithal, Ajit Kumar, Ambika Sharma (Institute of Chemical Technology, Mumbai; Bhavan's Hazarimal Somani College)
**Submitted**: August 4, 2026
**Categories**: cs.LG, math.AT (TDA)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03891
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Proposes a kernel k-means clustering algorithm built on a convex combination of sliced Wasserstein (SW) kernels, one per homology dimension (H₀, H₁, H₂). SW kernels are both stable and discriminative with respect to the 1-Wasserstein distance between persistence diagrams. Avoids full Wasserstein distance computation (expensive) while retaining topological discriminability. Outperforms baselines on benchmark TDA clustering datasets.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:
- **No cyclic group or orbit structure**: This paper clusters arbitrary point clouds or data complexes using topological persistence. QA's orbit classification (Cosmos/Satellite/Singularity) is a partition of Z/24Z by σ-transition dynamics, not a clustering problem. There is no modular arithmetic, no cyclic group structure, and no discrete dynamical system in this paper.
- **Contrast with scan #4 high-value paper**: arXiv:2605.06352 (scan #4, HIGH) specifically showed that H₁ persistent homology of neural network activations = cyclic orbit signature for modular arithmetic networks. This paper's technical contribution (SW kernel k-means) is a computational convenience for generic TDA tasks, not a structural result about cyclic groups or orbit theory.
- **Verdict**: LOW. Generic TDA clustering; no structural QA overlap. No cert action.

### 2. arXiv:2608.04308 — "Model theory of convolution algebras"

**Authors**: Alexander Berenstein, Kyle Gannon, Shichang Song
**Submitted**: August 5, 2026
**Categories**: math.LO (Logic), math.FA (Functional Analysis)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04308
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Studies the model theory (first-order logic) of convolution algebras (ℓ^1(G), *) for locally compact groups G. Key result: transfer principles for elementary equivalence and elementary embeddings when G is discrete — (ℓ^1(G), *) ≡ (ℓ^1(H), *) implies G ≡ H (with saturation conditions). For non-discrete G, the convolution product renders the algebra "wild" (unstable formula d(x, x*y)). 34 pages.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:
- **Wrong kind of "model theory"**: The paper is about logical model theory (elementary equivalence, first-order definability in Banach lattices), not about dynamical models or physical systems. The convolution algebra here is ℓ^1(G) as a Banach lattice equipped with group convolution — a functional analysis object.
- **Z/24Z triviality**: For a finite discrete group G = Z/24Z, ℓ^1(Z/24Z) = ℂ^24 with circular convolution. The paper's main results address locally compact non-discrete cases and logical classification of the abstract algebra. No orbit classification, no modular dynamics, no cyclic group periodicity content.
- **No structural overlap with QA**: The paper's logical transfer principle is about abstract algebraic equivalence between group algebras, not about orbit dynamics or the QA state space.
- **Verdict**: LOW. Abstract model theory of Banach convolution algebras; no cyclic dynamics or modular arithmetic content. No cert action.

### 4. arXiv:2606.26212 — "A General Framework for Learning Algebraic Properties from Cayley Graphs using Graph Neural Networks" — MEGA-SLIP

**Authors**: (Fokam cluster / related to IGE group — details unresolved due to egress block)
**Submitted**: ~June 26, 2026
**Categories**: cs.LG, math.GR
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26212
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: MEGA-SLIP — missed scans #17–26 (9 consecutive scans)

**Summary**: Proposes a unified GNN pipeline for learning algebraic properties of finite groups from their Cayley graph representations. Unified components: common graph construction procedure, feature representation, training methodology, and GNN architecture; only the target labeling varies. Demonstrates framework on three representative algebraic properties: abelianity, nilpotency, solvability. Benchmark: 176 finite groups from several classical families. Results show strong generalization to unseen group families. 

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:
- **Z/24Z is abelian — in scope**: Z/24Z is a finite abelian cyclic group of order 24. The paper's abelianity classification task correctly labels Z/24Z as abelian. Z/24Z is also nilpotent (all abelian groups are nilpotent) and solvable. So QA's primary modulus group IS in scope for all three tasks.
- **Cayley graph ↔ QA orbit graph**: QA's Cosmos orbit (72 pairs under σ-generator) is not the same as the full Cayley graph of Z/24Z. The Cayley graph Cay(Z/24Z, {σ}) is the 24-node cycle; QA's state space is the pair graph. However, the paper's framework operates on group Cayley graphs, and the 24-cycle IS a Cayley graph of Z/24Z.
- **Structural connection to IGE chain**: The scan #24 IGE cluster (arXiv:2606.29391 + 2607.13338) established that QA Cosmos orbit σ-transition graph embeds isometrically into Cayley(Z/24Z), and GE-GFT on this host = DFT = QA orbit character transform. This paper extends the chain: GNN trained on Cayley(Z/24Z) learns that Z/24Z is abelian/nilpotent/solvable. No new certifiable QA claim, but adds a third evidence layer to the Cayley graph representation learning chain.
- **Not a standalone cert**: The paper classifies GROUP PROPERTIES from Cayley graphs — it doesn't certify anything about QA's orbit partition, character structure, or modular dynamics. The connection is via the Cayley graph substrate, not via the orbital decomposition.
- **Root cause of mega-slip**: Cross-listed cs.LG/math.GR; not caught by either category sweep. The paper was submitted ~June 26, 2026 (end of scan #17 window June 25–July 2). Should have appeared in scan #17 or #18.
- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. GNN algebraic property learning from Cayley graphs; Z/24Z in scope; extends IGE evidence chain. No standalone cert candidate. Adds to `qa_graph_orbit_ge_gft_cert_v1` [541] evidence pool.

### 5. arXiv:2607.12026 — "Learning the Graphical Nature of Symmetries" — MEGA-SLIP

**Authors**: Rashid Barket, Enrico Grimaldi, Yacoub Hendi, Edward Hirst, Adam Onus, Harmeet Singh
**Submitted**: July 13, 2026
**Categories**: stat.ML, cs.LG, math.CO, math.GR
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12026
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: MEGA-SLIP — missed scans #20–26 (6 consecutive scans)

**Summary**: Constructs a comprehensive dataset of 131,406 Cayley graphs covering **all finite groups of order ≤ 767** (except order 512). For each group, records exact algebraic labels (abelian, nilpotent, solvable, simple, cyclic, etc.) together with a broad collection of graph statistics: graph properties, cycle structure, distance statistics, and **spectral statistics** (Laplacian/adjacency eigenvalues). Contributes new OEIS sequences for monolithic groups and for groups generated by at most 3, 4, and 5 elements.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:
- **Z/24Z is explicitly in scope**: Z/24Z has order 24 ≤ 767. The dataset includes its Cayley graph, algebraic labels (abelian ✓, nilpotent ✓, solvable ✓, cyclic ✓, simple ✗), and spectral statistics.
- **Spectral statistics ↔ QA orbit DFT**: The "spectral statistics" for Cayley(Z/24Z, {generator}) are the eigenvalues of the adjacency/Laplacian matrix of the 24-cycle. For the standard generator σ (step +1), these are the 24th roots of unity — exactly the DFT eigenvalues of the Z/24Z orbit character decomposition established in scan #5 (arXiv:2605.11589) and scan #17 (arXiv:2606.23044). This dataset provides independent external tabulation of the spectral structure underlying QA's orbit DFT.
- **Limitation — generator dependence**: The dataset's Cayley graph depends on the choice of generating set. If the paper uses a canonical generator or multiple generators, Z/24Z's spectral structure may be computed for a different generator than QA's σ-step. The spectral statistics for Cay(Z/24Z, {+1}) are definite (24-cycle), but other generators give different spectra.
- **OEIS contribution adjacent to QA**: New sequences for "groups generated by at most 3 elements" — Z/24Z is generated by 1 element (cyclic), so it appears in the "1-generator" case.
- **Cross-4-category coverage explains mega-slip**: stat.ML + cs.LG + math.CO + math.GR — this paper falls into a multi-category blind spot that no single search catches.
- **Not a standalone cert**: Provides external spectral data validation for Z/24Z Cayley graph; no new certifiable QA orbit dynamics claim. Informs human tract of `qa_graph_orbit_ge_gft_cert_v1` [541].
- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. Comprehensive external spectral dataset; Z/24Z ∈ scope; DFT eigenvalues tabulated. No standalone cert. Extends IGE/orbit character chain as validation dataset. Add to [541] evidence pool.

**Authors**: Tal Weissblat
**Submitted**: July 2, 2026
**Categories**: math.GR, cs.LG
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02102
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: MEGA-SLIP — missed scans #18–26 (8 consecutive scans)

**Summary**: Trains GNNs on finite complete and truncated Cayley graphs to predict group-theoretic growth type (polynomial vs. exponential). Evaluates without retraining on infinite finitely generated groups: free abelian groups Z^d, discrete Heisenberg group, infinite dihedral group, free groups, direct products. - **Cayley graph used for structural, not dynamical, purposes**: The paper uses Cayley graphs as geometric objects encoding the group's word metric geometry. QA uses the σ-transition Cayley graph for orbit dynamics. Different purposes, different mathematical content.
- **Root cause of mega-slip**: math.GR primary category; submitted July 2 (first day of scan #18 window); not caught by any search.
- **Verdict**: LOW. GNN growth type prediction from Cayley graphs; no orbit classification or modular arithmetic content. No cert action.

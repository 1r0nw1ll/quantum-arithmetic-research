## New Finds This Window

### 1. arXiv:2608.19507 — "On the number of solutions of systems of diagonal equations through diagonal GP-graphs: the general and the Hermitian-form cases"

**Authors**: Ricardo A. Podestá, Denis E. Videla
**Submitted**: August 19–20, 2026
**Categories**: math.NT (Number Theory), math.CO (Combinatorics)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19507
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Studies monic systems of diagonal equations over finite fields F_q of the form X_1^{k_1} + ... + X_s^{k_s} = β_i (1 ≤ i ≤ m). Introduces "diagonal GP-graphs": Cayley graphs Γ(κ,q) = Cay(F_q^m, R_κ) where R_κ = {(x^{k_1},...,x^{k_m}) : x ∈ F_q*} is generated by powers of the multiplicative group F_q*. Provides three equivalent expressions for the number of solutions: (1) number of walks in Γ(κ,q), (2) trace of powers of the adjacency matrix, (3) spectral decomposition using eigenvalues of Γ(κ,q). Sections 5–8 develop the Hermitian-form specialization where the exponent vector κ has a Hermitian symmetry.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:

- **Cayley graph machinery**: QA's orbit structure on Z/24Z × Z/24Z can be viewed as a Cayley graph (the σ-transition map generates a graph). The diagonal GP-graph framework is structurally parallel — Cayley graphs of algebraic structures, spectrum-based counting. This adds evidence to the GE-GFT cluster [541].

- **Diagonal coordinate resonance**: QA's canonical coordinates d = b+e and a = b+2e are *linear* diagonal functions of (b,e). The paper studies *power-based* diagonal equations X^k = β. These are different (linear vs monomial), but both exploit the diagonal / coordinate-change structure that QA uses. The mapping is loose.

- **Finite field vs ring obstruction**: The paper works over F_q (prime power order finite fields). QA operates over Z/24Z = Z/8Z × Z/3Z by CRT. Z/8Z is not a field (zero divisors at 2, 4, 6); Z/3Z = GF(3) is a field. The spectral theory for Cayley graphs over fields uses character sum methods (Gauss sums, Jacobi sums) that do not transfer cleanly to ring-valued settings. This is the primary limitation.

- **Multiplicative group structure**: R_κ is built from F_q*, the multiplicative group of order q−1. QA's unit group U(Z/24Z) = {1,5,7,11,13,17,19,23} has order 8 = φ(24). The parallel — using the multiplicative symmetry group to generate the Cayley set — is structurally valid, but the arithmetic differs (field units are cyclic; ring units may not be).

- **Hermitian form case**: The Hermitian specialization in Sections 5–8 generalizes quadratic forms. QA has explicit quadratic identities (b*b, b*b+e*e, etc.) — this section is the most QA-adjacent part of the paper, but the field setting still limits direct transfer.

- **Connection to [541]**: The paper extends the Cayley-graph-meets-algebraic-structure thread. The GE-GFT cluster [541] (arXiv:2606.29391, 2607.13338) already covers Cayley graph embeddings for signal processing. This paper contributes the *spectral counting* angle — how many orbits/solutions exist, not just their structure. This is complementary, making it evidence for [541] but not a standalone cert candidate.

- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. Structurally adjacent to [541] cluster; the diagonal/spectral/Cayley triple is QA-relevant machinery even though the algebraic setting differs. Worth noting as supporting evidence for [541]. No standalone cert candidate — the field vs ring gap is too wide for a direct mapping.

### 2. arXiv:2608.20191 — "Spectrum of the refined Diophantine exponent"

**Authors**: Not confirmed in search results
**Submitted**: August 20, 2026
**Categories**: math.CO (Combinatorics), cs.FL (Formal Languages), math.DS (Dynamical Systems), math.NT (Number Theory)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20191
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Studies the "refined Diophantine exponent" of infinite words — a quantity measuring the periodicity/repetition quality of a word. Proves: (1) Over a ternary alphabet, the spectrum of this exponent is [1,∞]; (2) The exponent has topological properties similar to Liouville numbers. Provides concrete examples: Champernowne word, Rudin-Shapiro word, Thue-Morse word, words from interval-exchange codings, bracket words.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:

- **No cyclic group / modular orbit structure**: The refined Diophantine exponent measures approximation quality of real numbers by rationals via continued fractions — a real-analysis / Diophantine approximation tool. QA time is integer path length; QA states are elements of Z/24Z × Z/24Z. These are entirely different domains.

- **Thue-Morse angle**: The Thue-Morse sequence has modular structure (digit sum mod 2). However, the paper uses Thue-Morse only as a benchmark example; it does not analyze its modular structure, only its approximation exponent. No direct QA mapping.

- **Interval exchange codings**: "Words from coding a rotation by intervals" are related to Rauzy sequences and Sturmian words. QA has Sturmian-adjacent structure (Fibonacci matrix cert [291]). However, the Diophantine exponent as studied here focuses on real-number approximation quality, not discrete combinatorial structure.

- **Verdict**: LOW. Real-analysis / infinite-word setting; no structural cyclic group or modular orbit content. No cert action.

## New Finds This Window

### 1. arXiv:2608.08118 — "Neurosymbolic Discovery of Algebraic Graph Constructions"

**Authors**: David Seka, Stefan Szeider (Vienna, TU Wien)
**Submitted**: August 8, 2026
**Venue**: NeSy 2026 (20th Conference on Neurosymbolic Learning and Reasoning, PMLR vol. 284)
**Categories**: cs.SC (Symbolic Computation), cs.AI
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08118
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: New in window (published August 8, within scan #29 sweep)

**Summary**: Proposes an LLM-based agent that interleaves natural-language reasoning with calls to the computer algebra system SageMath to automatically discover short algebraic descriptions of graphs from raw adjacency data. Given a target graph as input, the agent asks: "Is this a Cayley graph? A lexicographic product? A circulant?" — then proposes candidate descriptions and verifies them algebraically. The system requires no fine-tuning or per-target training. The research question is: can we extract structural algebraic content from raw graph data without human inspection?

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:

- **Cayley graph recognition is directly relevant to QA's orbit structure**: QA's σ-transition graph on Z/24Z pairs is a Cayley graph of Z/24Z. The paper's agent, given QA's orbit graph as input, would in principle recognize it as a Cayley graph and output the group and generator. This is an automated version of what scan #24's IGE chain (arXiv:2606.29391 et al.) establishes theoretically: QA's orbit graph embeds isometrically into Cayley(Z/24Z).

- **Extends Cayley graph ML cluster to 8 papers**: This is the eighth paper in the cluster established in scans #24 (IGE framework) and #27 (dataset + solvability GNNs). Previous papers classified algebraic properties from Cayley graphs; this paper DISCOVERS algebraic descriptions of graphs. New methodological angle: synthesis vs. classification.

- **Neurosymbolic framing adds value for QA cert pipeline**: The agent uses SageMath — the same computer algebra system available for QA cert validation. If the neurosymbolic discovery approach is applied to QA's functional graph, it could automate the algebraic characterization work needed for [261] human tract and cert [541].

- **Limitation — no modular dynamics content**: The paper discovers "this is a Cayley graph of G with generator S" — it does not characterize orbit dynamics, character decompositions, or cyclic period structures. The discovery is topological/structural, not dynamical.

- **Not a standalone cert**: Adds a new methodological angle to the Cayley graph learning cluster. No certifiable QA-specific claim.

- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. Neurosymbolic Cayley graph discovery; extends [541] evidence pool with a synthesis axis. Add to Honorable Mentions.

### 2. arXiv:2608.12526 — "Cyclic Shuffle Groups: Universal Two-Transitivity and Complete Classification"

**Author**: Benjamin Marsh
**Submitted**: August 12, 2026
**Categories**: math.CO (Combinatorics), math.GR (Group Theory)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12526
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Cyclic shuffle groups H_{k,n} are permutation groups generated by all k-pile cyclic shuffle permutations of a deck of kn cards (k piles of n cards each). The paper proves: if n ≠ k^f for any f ≥ 1, then H_{k,n} is 2-transitive; and gives the complete classification: H_{k,n} ≅ C_k ≀ C_{f+1} when n = k^f; H_{4,n} ≅ AGL(2j+3, 2) when n = 2·4^j; and H_{k,n} = Alt(kn) or Sym(kn) otherwise (determined by generator parity). Proof uses residual commutators and strongly connected digit digraphs.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:

- **Numerical coincidences only**: For k=3, n=24 (not a power of 3), H_{3,24} = Alt(72) or Sym(72) — acting on 72 elements, the size of QA's Cosmos orbit. For k=8, n=3, H_{8,3} = Alt(24) or Sym(24) — acting on 24 elements, QA's modulus. These numerical matches are not structural: the cyclic shuffle group acts on a card deck via combinatorial permutations, not on a cyclic group Z/nZ via the σ-transition.

- **Card shuffling ≠ QA modular dynamics**: H_{k,n} encodes the algebraic structure of card rearrangements. QA's σ-transition encodes modular addition on the pair space Z/24Z × Z/24Z. These are mathematically unrelated despite sharing degree parameters.

- **No orbit dynamics content**: The classification theorem characterizes the abstract group type (Alt/Sym vs. wreath product) but gives no information about orbit classification, character decomposition, or modular periodicity. The 24-element and 72-element coincidences are superficial.

- **Verdict**: LOW. Card-shuffling combinatorics; numerical coincidences with QA orbit sizes are not structural. No cert action.

### 3. arXiv:2608.12758 — "Cyclic Permutations of Large Subsets with Polynomial Values in Multiplicative Subgroups of Finite Fields"

**Authors**: Hai-Liang Wu, He-Xia Ni
**Submitted**: August ~14, 2026
**Categories**: math.NT (Number Theory), math.CO (Combinatorics)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12758
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Studies cyclic permutations of large subsets S ⊆ F_q such that the evaluations of a polynomial f on S land within a prescribed multiplicative subgroup of F_q*. Proves existence and size bounds for such subsets in terms of the multiplicative subgroup index and polynomial degree.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:

- **Field setting vs. composite ring setting**: The paper works over F_q (finite fields: prime-power order). QA's primary moduli are Z/9Z and Z/24Z — composite rings, not fields. Z/24Z is not a field (it has zero divisors: 3×8=0 mod 24). The paper's results about multiplicative subgroups of F_q* do not directly apply.

- **Thematic adjacency to arXiv:2608.11414 (scan #28 MEDIUM-HIGH)**: Both scan #28's MEDIUM-HIGH paper and this paper work with unit/multiplicative group actions on cyclic structures. But 2608.11414 worked with Z/nZ units (directly applicable to Z/24Z), while this paper works with F_q* (fields only). The adjacency is thematic, not structural.

- **φ(24) = 8 side note**: The multiplicative group U(Z/24Z) = {1,5,7,11,13,17,19,23} has order φ(24) = 8. If this paper's polynomial value results could be lifted to the ring Z/24Z setting (e.g., via CRT: U(Z/24Z) ≅ U(Z/8Z) × U(Z/3Z)), there could be relevance. But such a lift is not in scope of the paper.

- **Verdict**: LOW. Finite field polynomial-value permutations; composite ring setting inapplicable. No cert action.

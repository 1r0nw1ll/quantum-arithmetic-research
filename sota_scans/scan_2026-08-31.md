### 1. arXiv:2608.28141 — "Lattice Green's function of the hyperkagome lattice: modular uniformization at level 30 from an orthogonal differential Galois group"

**Authors**: Bryan Nasr, Jean-Marie Maillard
**Submitted**: August 28, 2026
**Categories**: cond-mat.stat-mech (Statistical Mechanics), math-ph (Mathematical Physics)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28141
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window

**Summary**: Computes the lattice Green's function for the hyperkagome lattice (the iridium sublattice of spin-liquid candidate Na₄Ir₃O₈). Starting from exact lattice moments, the authors derive an irreducible third-order linear Picard-Fuchs differential operator annihilating the Green's function. The differential Galois group is orthogonal (SO(3) type), and an order-two intertwiner makes the operator projectively a symmetric square. The underlying second-order operator is the uniformizing equation of the genus-zero modular curve Γ₀(30)+, the level-30 group generated by Γ₀(30) and all its Atkin-Lehner involutions. The paper resolves a threefold integral that had resisted exact evaluation for years.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:

- **"Modular" ≠ Z/nZ**: The paper's "modular" refers to the modular curve Γ₀(30)+ — the Atkin-Lehner quotient of the classical congruence subgroup at level 30. This is in the automorphic forms sense of "modular," entirely distinct from QA's Z/24Z or Z/9Z modular arithmetic. The word "modular" here names a class of complex-analytic functions, not remainder arithmetic.

- **"Galois group" = differential Galois theory**: The "orthogonal differential Galois group" is the Galois group of the Picard-Fuchs ODE's function field extension — a continuous Lie group measuring algebraic independence of ODE solutions. This is not the inverse Galois problem (realizing a group as Gal(K/Q)) and has no connection to SAIR IGP24 or degree-24 orbit structure.

- **Level 30 vs. QA moduli**: QA uses Z/24Z (mod 24, applied) and Z/9Z (mod 9, theoretical). Level 30 = 2 × 3 × 5 has no structural connection to either modulus.

- **Condensed matter physics context**: The paper's domain is spin-liquid physics and lattice statistical mechanics — completely unrelated to QA's signal processing, finance, neural network, or geometry applications.

- **Verdict**: LOW. Triple false cognate: "modular" (automorphic sense), "Galois group" (differential sense), and "level 30" (has no Z/24Z or Z/9Z connection). No cert action.

### 2. arXiv:2608.14524 — "The inverse Galois problem of iterated Galois groups and their fixed-point proportion"

**Authors**: Santiago Radi
**Submitted**: mid-August 2026 (precise date ~Aug 11–15; first surfaced in scan #33 web search)
**Categories**: math.NT (Number Theory), math.GR (Group Theory)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14524
**Rating**: LOW-MEDIUM
**Status**: New in window (or at boundary of scan #32 window)

**Summary**: A 54-page paper on arithmetic statistics for iterated polynomial maps. Studies iterated Galois groups Gal(f^n(X) - a / K) for a polynomial f over a number field K, and the density of elements with fixed-point-free (or fixed-point) action in these groups as n → ∞. Connects to the Odoni conjecture on the image of the arboreal representation and extends probabilistic Galois theory (Ellenberg-Elsholtz-Hall-Kowalski type results) to the iterated setting.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW-MEDIUM**:

- **"Inverse Galois" label is misleading**: Despite containing "inverse Galois problem" in the title, the paper is about the distribution of Galois groups of iterated polynomial maps, not about realizing specific groups as Gal(K/Q). The "inverse Galois problem" referred to is the question of whether all finite groups arise as iterated Galois groups — a statistical inverse Galois question, not the constructive one SAIR IGP24 addresses.

- **Adjacency to [506]**: SAIR cert [506] (`qa_inverse_galois_degree24_cert_v1`) documents the degree-24 inverse Galois competition. This paper's connection to [506] is through shared vocabulary ("inverse Galois") rather than shared mathematical content. A background citation in [506]'s human tract is possible if the paper discusses degree-24 extensions, but no evidence of that from search results.

- **Orbit structure of iterated maps**: The paper studies orbits of Galois groups acting on rooted trees (arboreal representations). This is a form of orbit classification, but for infinite trees arising from polynomial iteration, not finite cyclic groups Z/24Z. The structure of arboreal Galois groups (profinite, not finite) is different from QA's finite orbit tripartition.

- **Fixed-point proportion**: The fixed-point-free proportion connects to cycle counting in group actions — adjacent to QA's orbit structure analysis. But the setting (polynomial iteration over number fields, tree-structured Galois groups) is structurally distinct from QA's linear map σ on (Z/24Z)².

- **Verdict**: LOW-MEDIUM. Title creates false proximity to SAIR [506]; content is probabilistic arithmetic statistics for polynomial iteration. Orbit-counting language is superficially adjacent to QA but arises in a completely different setting (arboreal/profinite vs. finite cyclic). No standalone cert action. Could add as a context note in [506]'s human tract only if review confirms degree-24 coverage — not established from web search alone.

**Categories**: math.GR, math.DS, cs.FL
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20600

**Summary**: Proves a characterization theorem: a bijective linear cellular automaton V^G → V^G over a group G and a field K-vector space V admits an inverse that is NOT a cellular automaton if and only if G is not locally finite and dim_K V ≥ |K|^ℵ₀. - **Cross-listing math.GR + math.DS**: The cross-listing flagged this in the search battery, but the content is purely abstract (infinite groups, transfinite algebra).

- **Verdict**: LOW. Inapplicable to QA's finite cyclic group setting. No cert action.

### 5. arXiv:2608.03254 — "Cyclic-by-abelian counterexamples to the second and third Zassenhaus conjectures"

**Authors**: Brecht Verbeken
**Submitted**: ~August 2, 2026
**Categories**: math.GR (Group Theory), math.RA (Rings and Algebras)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03254
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window (early August, near scan #32 boundary)

**Summary**: Provides counterexamples to the second and third Zassenhaus conjectures within the class of cyclic-by-abelian groups. The Zassenhaus conjectures concern torsion units in the integral group ring ZG of a finite group G: (1st) all torsion units are rationally conjugate to group elements; (2nd) all finite subgroups are rationally conjugate to subgroups of G; (3rd) extensions of finite subgroups are rationally conjugate to extensions in G. First conjecture was proved for cyclic-by-abelian groups in 2012; this paper shows the second and third fail for this class.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:

- **Group rings vs. group dynamics**: QA's structure is a dynamical system on Z/24Z (additive group with σ-transition), not a module structure for the integral group ring Z[Z/24Z]. The Zassenhaus conjectures are about algebraic K-theory of group rings — entirely orthogonal to QA's orbit-theoretic setting.

- **"Cyclic-by-abelian"**: While QA uses cyclic and abelian groups, the term "cyclic-by-abelian" here means a group G with a normal cyclic subgroup N such that G/N is abelian (an extension class). This is a group-theoretic construction unrelated to Z/24Z's ring structure or QA's dynamics.

- **Verdict**: LOW. Group ring algebra (Zassenhaus conjectures) has no structural overlap with QA's orbit dynamics. No cert action.

### 6. arXiv:2608.21084 — "Abelian maximal pattern complexity of two-dimensional words"

**Authors**: Qingcheng Zeng, Yumei Xue, Cheng Zeng
**Submitted**: August 21, 2026
**Categories**: math.CO (Combinatorics), math.DS (Dynamical Systems)
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21084
**Rating**: LOW
**Status**: New in window (scan #32 boundary)

**Summary**: Studies the maximal pattern complexity of two-dimensional infinite words up to abelian equivalence (two words are abelian-equivalent if they are letter permutations of each other). Establishes a lower bound for abelian maximal pattern complexity of 2D words that are nondoubly periodic by projection, under recurrence conditions. The bound is sharp; gives the exact complexity for strongly recurrent binary non-doubly periodic words and a double-periodicity criterion.

**QA Mapping Assessment — LOW**:

The "abelian" in this paper refers to this combinatorial equivalence relation, not to Z/24Z being an abelian group.

- **"Two-dimensional words"**: These are functions from ℤ² to a finite alphabet — totally unrelated to QA's 2D state space (Z/24Z)² of (b,e) pairs.

- **Pattern complexity vs. orbit complexity**: QA's orbit structure (Cosmos 72 pairs, Satellite 8 pairs, Singularity 1 pair) is an orbit classification under the σ-transition, not a pattern complexity measure. The paper's complexity measures (counting distinct patterns in balls of radius k) are adjacent to symbolic dynamics and word combinatorics, not to QA's algebraic orbit tripartition.

- **Verdict**: LOW. False cognates for "abelian" and "two-dimensional." No QA structural overlap. No cert action.
